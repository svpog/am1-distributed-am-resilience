# AM1 content-overlap audit

## Scope

The revised manuscript was compared with the four principal earlier works supplied by the authors. References sections were excluded where a clear heading was available. The check is an internal research-integrity audit, not a commercial similarity report.

## Conceptual separation

| Work | Main factor and evidence | Separation from AM1 |
|---|---|---|
| Article 1 | General supply-network topology, stochastic disruption, irreversibility and flow-based resilience metrics | AM1 uses typed AM technological stages, qualification-constrained relations, digital enabling dependencies and an equal structural intervention budget. No Article 1 table, figure or numerical result is reused. |
| Article 2 | Targeted fragility of hub networks and attack-rule sensitivity | AM1 does not compare hub-selection rules. Its targeted scenarios are role-specific feedstock and post-processing failures inside a typed AM chain. |
| Article 3 | Comparison of recovery policies and scale effects | AM1 fixes one flow-greedy recovery policy and varies architecture and disruption mechanism; recovery strategy is not an experimental factor. |
| Article 4 | Decentralized multi-agent recovery, visibility and coordination | AM1 has no agent-number, visibility-radius or coordination factor. It evaluates structural AM redundancy with a single fixed recovery rule. |

## Conclusion

The revised manuscript is substantively and textually distinct from the four earlier works. The earlier studies are cited only as methodological precursors. The new contribution is supported by a separate preregistered AM1 protocol, new code paths, disjoint seed namespaces, 15,000 new simulation runs, new tables and new figures. This audit does not replace the journal's institutional similarity system, which may use different databases and matching rules.
